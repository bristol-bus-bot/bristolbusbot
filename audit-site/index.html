        How it works

        The point. This site compares independent measurements with WECA's dated annual target and longer-term goal.
        What "on time" means

        Between 1 minute early and 5 minutes 59 seconds late at a timing point, the Department for Transport's statistical definition. A reading is a bus recorded within 150 m of a timing point. We measure every bus we can see, every day, against the published timetable.

        "Likely reasons" are suggestions, not facts

        They come from patterns in the data: a stretch where buses lose time on most days, a time of day that is much worse than the rest, journeys that go missing, or buses that wait at a stop. Each one shows its evidence. When there is no clear pattern, we don't suggest a reason.

        This is about the timetable and the service, not drivers

        Figures measure buses against their timetable. They are not a measure of individual drivers.

        What we do not claim
        • We do not claim any operator is breaking the law or withholding required data. Operators are legally required to publish timetables, vehicle locations and fares, and they do. Performance figures are not a statutory publication requirement.
        • We do not claim these are the official figures. They are an independent estimate and will differ from any internal operator or regulator measurement.
        • Low-sample routes, smaller operators with patchier real-time data, and partial collection days are not reliable on their own, and are flagged as such.
        Historical data note

        Some days have incomplete or contradictory evidence and are excluded. Method and timetable changes also limit comparisons across periods. See the dated change record.

        Full methodology and data

        The method, data sources (including routes drawn from TNDS that operators do not publish to BODS), the geography, fleet and frequent-service definitions, and every limitation are in AUDIT_METHODOLOGY.md. Day-by-day history is in audit_data.json.
